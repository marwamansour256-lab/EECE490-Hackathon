Why is profit growing?

Exact breakdown of gross profit = orders × items per order × price per item × margin. Bars: Jan→Mar (dark) and Apr→Jun (light). The same split in both halves means it is a stable pattern, not a one-off.

Why does Garden earn less than Harbour?

Garden's own sales, re-priced at Harbour's prices, then at Harbour's costs. Identical in Jan–Mar and Apr–Jun. Next step: compare Garden's supplier invoices with Harbour's.

Why do daily sales go up and down?

Each model is fitted on Jan–Mar and scored on Apr–Jun, which it never saw. The ceiling assumes customers arrive at random around the expected rate (Poisson). It is a benchmark for daily order counts, not an absolute limit. Outside factors can still change other outcomes, such as the time of day or product mix (see EXTERNAL_CONTEXT.md).

What is different about Tuesday?

The data cannot name the cause. A uniform lift points to something chain-wide (a calendar pattern, a standing offer, operations or how sales are recorded) rather than a type of customer or product. Ask Stories, then try to copy it on Saturdays (about 0.7× a normal weekday).

Source: src/why_drivers.py → app/why_data.js, reports/why_drivers.json. These are accounting breakdowns and held-out fits, not causal effects.

Where the profit gap comes from

Gross profit = orders × items per order × gross profit per item. The gap from the reference is split exactly into these three parts (a Shapley split, which doesn't depend on order). This is accounting, not proof of a cause. Reference: orders expected by the model trained on Jan–Mar; basket and profit per item from Mar 4–31.

Why the plan uses no outside factors (yet)

    
We tested whether weather, holidays, Ramadan, the war period and Beirut strike days help predict sales on days the model never saw.
       Each factor was added to a transactions-only model trained on one half of the timeline and scored on the other half, in both directions,
       against 154 placebo versions with the factor moved to the wrong dates, with a correction for testing many factors.

    

      
0 of 33
factor tests pass

      
~20%
drop on rainy days or holidays that the test catches every time

      
~10%
drop it catches only 40–50% of the time

    

    
Meaning: no outside factor improves the forecast out of sample, and any rainy-day or holiday effect larger than about 20% would have been found.
       Smaller effects cannot be ruled out. Weather is realised weather (a retrospective test), branch locations are unverified, and conflict dates were picked after the fact.
       Source: src/external_factor_test.py, reports/external_factor_test.json.
