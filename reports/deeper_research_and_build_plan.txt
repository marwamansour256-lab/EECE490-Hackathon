THE PROPOSAL
Build a preparation decision engine with an evidence-linked event review layer.
Before opening, choose a batch for each branch/item. At 16:00, enter actual remaining
stock and receive a second-batch quantity. Charge the extra preparation work and
compare contribution and customer fulfillment. A manager can change the costs and
see when the proposed intervention stops making money.

The decision engine and CLI are implemented. A live operational feed and frontend
are not. The innovation in this submission is the auditable business decision and
its validation, not a claim that dynamic programming is a newly invented algorithm.
Inventory dynamic programming background (primary teaching source):
https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-231-dynamic-programming-and-stochastic-control-fall-2015/resources/mit6_231f15_lec4/

WHY NOT JUST ADD MORE CORRELATIONS?
The external-feature forecasting variants did not beat internal history overall.
One-off holidays, six months of weather/trend confounding and anonymous branches
make confident causal explanations impossible. Searching enough headlines after
choosing a sales spike will usually produce a plausible story. The implementation
therefore retains unsupported dates as unresolved and tests whether the proposed
mechanism matches the branch and time-of-day pattern.

New outlier audit: 2,224 comparisons = 4 scopes x 4 dayparts x 139 dates after
42 initial training days. Each forecast uses earlier observations only. Screened
Poisson/overdispersed count tails receive BH adjustment across all comparisons.
The approximate probabilities omit model-parameter uncertainty. Only Harbour
May 1 survives q<0.05 (149 versus ~100 orders; q~0.023). This is a screening result,
not proof of a Labour Day effect. All other large residuals require caution.
18 selected date dossiers include positive extremes, negative extremes and
event-first non-extreme comparisons; they are not independent confirmation tests.

EXTERNAL MECHANISMS THAT WERE INVESTIGATED
1. Physical access, not just celebration. ISF's February 13 notice establishes
   February 14 downtown closures. Harbour orders are 28.9% below rolling expected
   demand while Uptown is 1.5% above. Without branch coordinates, one cannot label
   Harbour downtown or assign the decline to access. Obtain road/entrance and
   opening-hours exposure. If verified, the action is a smaller opening batch or
   a delivery-access review, not an automatic Valentine's promotion.
   Exact Arabic source URL: data/external/outlier_event_registry.json, hariri_access.

2. Events can obstruct as well as attract. ISF announced the May 23 morning
   waterfront race on May 22. Network sales were down 7.9%, and evening orders
   were down more than morning orders. This contradicts a simple rule that a
   nearby sports event should automatically increase all-day preparation.
   Source: the same registry, one_run. Route-to-branch exposure is still missing.

3. Graduation is an hour-specific hypothesis. AUB lists June 5 at 19:30, with
   guests from 18:30. That day has +14.5% evening versus +4.7% morning orders.
   This is temporally compatible, but other June events and catchments matter.
   Haigazian's June 8 report also confirms its June 5 commencement. The latter
   article could not have been a June 5 forecast input. No numerical uplift is
   applied. Collect announcements before the event and campus distances, then
   evaluate future event days against comparable non-event days.
   https://www.aub.edu.lb/commencement/Pages/graduate-ceremony.aspx
   https://www.haigazian.edu.lb/2026/06/08/class-of-2026-bids-farewell-to-haigazian-university-president-haidostian-you-are-what-you-count-in-life/

4. Clock changes provide a timing falsification check. Government memo March 16
   schedules the clock advance at midnight March 28/29. It cannot explain the
   March 28 daytime spike. POS timezone semantics must be established before
   treating changes in recorded hour as changes in behavior.
   https://pcm.gov.lb/arabic/subpg.aspx?pageid=26984

5. War is a spatial and operational exposure, not one national dummy. Conflict
   was reported before the March 2 escalation and after the April 17 temporary
   ceasefire. June 24 waterfront displacement reporting coincides with a decline
   concentrated in Uptown and morning sales. This does not identify customer
   movement or a causal effect. A receiving area may gain customers while an
   inaccessible area loses them. Required features: local incident timing,
   distance, affected access routes, store closure hours and displacement-zone
   exposure, joined only after alias mapping. Do not reverse-engineer identities.
   Sources: external_evidence.json and outlier_event_registry.json.

6. Airport traffic is a macro context check. Banque du Liban's airport-arrival
   series reports Jan 235,497; Feb 209,351; Mar 48,994; Apr 72,454; May 186,829.
   The retrieved series supplies five monthly values, not a daily tourism signal.
   The March collapse alongside growing aggregate cafe transactions warns against
   assuming all businesses track tourism. It does not show that tourism has no
   effect. June is absent from this retrieved series. Publication vintage is not
   established, so these values are not used in operational forecasting.
   https://www.bdl.gov.lb/keyfiguressub.php?code=3&docId=9&filecode=323

7. Official exchange rates cannot explain daily variation here. The BDL monthly
   USD averages are 89,500 LBP for every month January-June. A constant regressor
   supplies no identifying variation. This says nothing about unobserved parallel
   rates, household liquidity or actual transaction currency.
   https://www.bdl.gov.lb/economicandfinancialdatasub.php?code=9&docId=161&filecode=90

8. Fuel and cost-of-living shocks remain unqualified. Searches yielded isolated
   fuel announcements, not a verified vintage-aware daily sequence matched to
   branch transport exposure. A May 15 announcement cannot explain May 14 sales
   without separate evidence of anticipation. Do not fill missing dates with
   guessed prices. Monthly CPI copied across days does not create daily evidence.

9. Weather and seasons: actual Beirut weather is a geographic proxy. The original
   team weather notebook and context models include category and branch checks.
   The new forecasting comparison distinguishes lagged weather from realized
   future-weather oracle features. Neither tested external family wins overall.
   Temperature, elapsed time, school calendar and conflict overlap in six months;
   a winter/spring/early-summer table is descriptive, not annual seasonal learning.

10. Holiday effects differ by item. Across four Muslim closure episodes (nine
    dates), sandwich demand is about four units/day lower across the network in
    adjusted exploratory models. Leaving out one episode keeps the direction;
    four event blocks give a two-sided sign-test p=0.125. Turkey & Cheese and
    Chicken Pesto show negative estimates, while Tuna does not follow that sign.
    These product comparisons were selected after inspecting the same data.
    They motivate item-specific holiday review, not automatic religious targeting
    or a validated forecast multiplier. Christian and Muslim holiday windows
    overlap different seasons, weekdays and conflict periods; no head-to-head
    causal religious ranking is identified.
    See holiday_robustness.json and external_evidence.json for exact sources/results.

11. Unobserved operational alternatives: POS outage, changed opening hours,
    product stockouts, a paid campaign, delivery-app ranking, competitor closure,
    local construction, power/internet interruption and group orders can produce
    outliers. No records establish their occurrence at the anonymized branches.
    These are specific fields to obtain, not explanations asserted as facts.

MODEL SPECIFICATION
Forecast candidate A: pooled regularized Poisson regression with branch/category,
weekday interactions, trend, lag-1, lag-7, past 7/28-day history and matching-weekday
features; category forecasts are allocated using prior item shares. Original
training is Jan-Mar, selection Apr-May and rolling June evaluation with weekly
refits. Candidate B: four matching weekdays with the same item allocation.
Original category WAPE: A 23.21%, B 24.79%. This accuracy win is not a profit win.

Optimizer: daily mean mu is split into early/late means using earlier 56-day
branch/category intraday shares, shrunk toward prior category shares. Demand is
conditionally independent Poisson by stage. The opening decision q0 maximizes
expected contribution including the optimal later decision q1(remaining stock).
Revenue is price times fulfilled demand; subtract ingredient costs, a fixed cost
per SKU second batch and extra cost per replenished unit; add stated salvage.
Default expected unit-fill floor is 90% for each stage, versus a daily 90% floor
for the one-batch comparator. The timing constraints differ and are disclosed.
No claim is made of 90% stockout-free days. No-service-floor is a stress scenario.
Prices/costs are quantity-weighted Jan-Mar branch-item economics, not future
realized item margins. The late action uses remaining stock, never future demand.

BUSINESS RESULT AND ITS LIMITS
With B, $0.25 per SKU replenishment, $0.10 per replenished unit and zero salvage:
one batch $4,930.92 contribution, two stages $5,341.65, difference $410.73.
Fulfillment 90.68% -> 93.43%; unsold 2,859 -> 2,763; unmet 434 -> 306 units.
920 SKU second batches means 10.2 per branch-day. Remaining cost headroom is
$4.56 per branch-day. At $1 setup + $0.25/unit, difference is -$486.49.
The original regression A earns $149.17 less than B under the same low-cost
two-stage policy. Its combined gain over B's one-batch plan is only $261.56.
The contribution improvement primarily comes from operational flexibility.

Breakdown for B in the low-cost case: sandwiches +$229.04, bakery +$139.38,
breakfast +$112.59, desserts -$70.28. These are exploratory category results;
choosing only winners after observing June would overstate future performance.
Prioritize verifying sandwich preparation feasibility; do not silently remove
dessert losses from the main result. If the company already makes to order or
replenishes continuously, these assumed inventory benefits may be negligible.

The replay treats recorded sales as demand and assumes immediate second-batch
availability, same-day carryover and no capacity restriction. Actual stockouts,
waste, batch labor and company policy are missing. The very large simulated
unsold quantities are model outputs, not evidence the company wastes that amount.
June was already examined when the policy was developed. All new policy results
are retrospective; intervals do not repair post-selection or missing data.

EXTERNAL ALERT CONTRACT AND FUTURE MODEL EXTENSION
For every event: event_id, source_url, published_at with timezone, event_start/end,
geometry or affected route, relevant branches, exposure evidence, mechanism,
expected affected hours/items, confidence status and analyst decision. Date-only
known_at fields in the current registry do not establish availability before that
day's opening. Unknown publication time means retrospective-only until resolved.

After enough exposed and unexposed future events accumulate, fit shrinkage
interactions for event exposure x branch x daypart/category. Compare the same
baseline and profit policy on a new future period. Estimate treatment effects
only with a defensible comparison design and parallel-trend/overlap checks.
With only three anonymous branches and one episode, do not call a national
before/after war comparison causal. Until validated, events trigger review only.

PROSPECTIVE PILOT AND REQUIRED COMPANY FIELDS
First shadow the planner while measuring: branch coordinates/timezone/open hours;
product shelf life; batch time, minimum batch and lead time; capacity by shift;
opening stock, prepared and replenished quantity/time; stockout intervals;
discarded quantity/reason; salvage; paid price, ingredient cost and extra labor;
current manager rule and overrides; promotions and closure/access exposure.

Then freeze the policy and eligible products, choose a business-relevant minimum
profit improvement, estimate required branch-day count from observed variance,
and randomize within branch and weekday to planner versus existing practice.
Account for carryover, shared labor and substitution; if these make branch-day
assignment unsuitable, revise the experimental unit before starting. Three
branches alone do not guarantee enough power. Set service and safety guardrails.

Primary outcome: incremental contribution after actual waste and incremental
labor/transport, using assignment-based comparison and uncertainty clustered by
the assignment unit with serial dependence handled. Secondary: fulfillment,
stockout minutes, discard units, labor minutes and manager override frequency.
Scale only if the business-relevant profit gain and service guardrails pass.
If costs exceed headroom or existing operations already capture the benefit,
reject this intervention instead of presenting simulated dollars as company ROI.
